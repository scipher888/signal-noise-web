@kaggle: Why can frontier AI models write complex code, yet still occasionally stumble when asked to identify the colors of five simple objects in order or verify what's not in an image?

That disconnect is called jagged intelligence and measuring it scientifically is critical to advancing AI reliability.

Today, @GoogleDeepMind is launching Humanity's First Exam on Kaggle, a benchmark designed to measure "floor reliability": whether models get simple tasks right every time, not just once. It spans 544 text and multimodal prompts across 20 foundational subcategories, in 136 four-prompt families.

Some early findings, across 18 frontier models:
• High single-shot accuracy masks inconsistency: Top five models reach 96%–98% pass@1 and 100% family pass@4, yet average only 85.6% on family pass^4 (80.5%–92.4%), with a 7.6–80.1 percentage-point Family Reliability Gap across all 18 models.
• Visual perception drives 74% of wrong answers: Extra thinking time helps far less on visual tasks than on text.
• Single-step slips are common: In 39% of failed prompt–model pairs, the answer is off by one or has a single wrong element; this holds for 167 of the 298 counting errors.
