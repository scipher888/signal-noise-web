Two thoughts I spend a lot of time with

Scale: Frontier, complete post-training is rapidly becoming less accessible due to resource requirements, similar to how pretraining looked at the beginning of 2023. This is solvable, but takes a lot of funding and commitment.

Lack of coverage: The (open) post-training literature has a lot of empty space in key ideas which will be relevant in 5+ years. It has been like this for years. It gives an exciting opportunity.

Lambert 5/51

What is post-training

Post-training is a more complete set of techniques and best practices to make base, next-token language models more useful for downstream tasks.

Three optimization methods: Supervised Finetuning (SFT), preference finetuning / human feedback / RLHF, and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

The term originated at OpenAI and was first used publicly in a prominent fashion with the release of GPT-4 in 2023:

“GPT-4 is a Transformer-based model pre-trained to predict the next token in a document. The post-training alignment process results in improved performance on measures of factuality and adherence to desired behavior.”

Lambert 6/51

Canonical recipes over time in post-training

The shape of a post-training recipe has changed more in the last year than in the prior three.

2022–2023 (InstructGPT): 3 stages — SFT → reward model → RL.

2024 (Llama 3, Tülu 3, etc.): maturing of InstructGPT, added more stages, added RLVR.

2025 (DeepSeek R1): reasoning makes large-scale RL the centerpiece.

2026 (MiMo-V2-Flash): recipes fragment into many specialist models that are merged back into one.

DeepSeek R1 (Jan. 2025) — the RL era

The recipe:

R1 — reasoning SFT/mid-training → reasoning RL → rejection-sampling SFT → final RL

Guo et al., 2025

Lambert 9/51

MOPD (2026) — specialization & on-policy distillation

Stages: Stage 1 SFT/reasoning foundation → Stage 2 train domain-specialist teachers (with older-style post-training recipes) → Stage 3 MOPD into a single student (final model).

Lineage: MiMo-V2-Flash introduced MOPD → DeepSeek V4, Nemotron 3 Ultra, and others use it as well (e.g. latest GPT models).

Specialist post-training with MOPD (RLHF Book, ch. 3) (Xiaomi LLM-Core Team et al., 2026)

Lambert 13/51

From post-training to post-post-training

Many companies and researchers start with the final reasoning model, rather than the base model.

Post-training: Building the core reasoning behavior of models. Now very expensive (O($50M+/yr) to do it well).

Post-post-training: Adapting a post-trained model to specific domains, efficiencies, capabilities, harnesses, etc.

Lambert 28/51

Major open problems in post-training

Reasoning efficiency in RL

+ manipulating CoT efficiency, and alignment

A simple idea (partially related): What if we spend more RL compute on the hardest problems?

Recall: The popular algorithm Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) generates multiple completions to the same prompts to try and create batches that have correct & incorrect answers (those have gradient).

Can we create batches dynamically around this to better allocate compute?

Lambert 41/51

Using LLMs accelerates output but very minimally accelerates understanding

More of science shifts to problem formulation, understanding “impact”, communication, standard setting, consensus building, mentorship, etc.

For senior scientists: I barely write code anymore, but I’m busier than ever as a scientist. LLMs are clearly very empowering.

For junior scientists: Doing inefficient work manually is often the only way to build understanding. Training could take longer, with more work feeling like “homework” rather than productive output.

Lambert 42/51

Examples of work that would be WAY faster: Zephyr β & Tülu 2

In 2023, it took a long time for clear, almost obvious, ideas to be used in strong models! In this era, the goal was to match performance of closed models on “chat” evals with minimal finetuning.

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) paper was released in May 2023 (Rafailov et al., 2023)

Zephyr β (Oct 2023) and Tülu 2 (Nov 2023) were released later, even though everyone was trying it.

Fun fact: Chris Manning thanked me for “saving” DPO with these two models.

Both these models came after a research dataset, UltraFeedback (Cui et al., 2023), was released in Oct 2023

The goals we have in building new research infrastructure & recipes:

Lambert 53/51

Where finetuning came from

Pretraining a model and then finetuning it was standard practice in computer vision before it took over NLP.

2018, GPT-1 and BERT: pretrain a language model on unlabeled text, then finetune it on each task (Radford et al., 2018) (Devlin et al., 2018)

2019, T5: every task cast as text-to-text, so the same model, objective, and decoding apply to all of them (Raffel et al., 2020)

2021, FLAN: instruction tuning, where finetuning on many tasks written as instructions improves zero-shot performance on unseen tasks (Wei et al., 2022)
