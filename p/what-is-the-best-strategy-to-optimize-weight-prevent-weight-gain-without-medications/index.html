What is the best strategy to optimize weight (prevent weight gain) without medications?

Written without AI. A stream-of-thought first pass, published verbatim.

A note on water: this describes the author's own habit, not a recommendation. Healthy kidneys can clear only about 0.8 to 1 liter of water an hour, and less for many people, so drinking large amounts quickly can be dangerous. Talk with your physician before making big changes to how much or how fast you drink. See the essay's water caution and sources.

Not exercise. You can’t exercise your way out of the calorie load you can acquire from eating. That one piece of cheesecake you ate? How do you like marathons? Because you may have to run one marathon (or more) to burn the 2,500+ calories in that delicious piece of cheesecake. So just count calories and keep them below a certain limit? Yes on paper. No in real life. Counting calories is effective and you can do this for short periods to meet some personal or athletic goal, but you will always feel tired and hungry and cranky and when you binge, watch out. How many calories can you eat in one sitting? 10,000 plus is not out of the question….or so I’ve heard. Turn out that thinking about & recording every calorie you eat and all the calories you would like to eat but are resisting all day long every day consumes a massive amount of willpower (which is limited) and keeps one focused on food - which makes it harder to resist. It is also an estimate of how many calories you actually need, and if you consume too few calories, you metabolism will actually slow down thus making it even harder (you need to eat less) to keep from gaining weight. So while it is possible and effective to do this for a short time, if you manage to do this for any sustained length of time (many months/years), you will almost certainly be hangry, tired, and depressed all the time. So Not a great long term strategy.

What then, if you can’t burn enough calories or constrain (limit) them by counting to ensure you don’t eat too much?

I don’t want to struggle with temptation. I don’t want to exercise my willpower and resist. I want to look with disdain at the most delicious impulse inducing food on the planet and turn away without effort - or even with disgust/repulsion/even nausea - I want it to be the easy choice, the gratifying & dopamine releasing choose to say no - as in “no, there is no way I can eat that” instead of “no, I would love to have that but I won’t”.

Well, I honestly don’t know the absolute best way, but what works for me is to focus on what I can control without draining my willpower and mojo/energy - which is to say I focus on optimizing my nutrition, as in what is the healthiest ~ 2,200 calorie diet in the entire world that I can eat right now? Obviously it doesn’t need to be that healthy but this is the starting point. What does a healthy diet look like for me based on an estimate (guesstimate) of my daily caloric requirements? Once I build and implement this diet (mine is whole plant foods based loosely on Dr. Gregor’s daily dozen and the institute for health metrics global burden of disease study.), the next step Is to stop eating at a certain time (I try to stop by 3 pm), and finally, aim to drink ~ 1 gallon of water a day - preferably about 1/4 gallon of water/1 quart/4 cups with each meal (check with your doctor first - this is a lot of water that could cause problems for some people). I actually drink more (at least 1/2 gallon with my last meal), and when I only have two meals a day (common), I usually consume an entire gallon of water during and after last meal some, but it usually takes at least 3 hours to finish drinking all this water right after a meal, but I can’t advise others to do this be because if you are drinking an entire gallon, it is safest to drink no more than. ~ 3 cups per hour (so the enter gallon should take ~ 5.5 hours). In any case, optimizing your daily nutrition (healthy diet that meets your caloric requirements) and combining the last meal (or last two meals) with a large amount of water and ideally a lot of fiber about 6 hours before bedtime will make you feel extremely full (sometimes even slightly nauseated) and pack your intestines with fiber in a way that keeps you feeling full (or too full) for ~ 6 hours (ideally until bedtime).

And then stop counting calories, be nice to yourself, accept that you will sometimes have an extra snack and/or even binge once or a few times a week, and trust your body to figure the rest out. When you optimize your nutrition, last meal timing, and water intake strategically in this way, your food cravings are much, much diminished, you spend most of the day quite full (or even too full/stuffed), and when you do snack or even binge, you just can’t to anywhere near as much damage as when you are counting calories and/or trying to exercise your way out of excessive calories intake.

All in all, it’s much, much, more sustainable long term approach to optimizing your health, weight, energy, and mood than counting calories and focusing on restriction/constraining your intake and/or trying to exercise to burn off all the extra calories you are eating.
