“The higher your intelligence, the better you are at fooling yourself. While it might seem natural to assume that the higher your IQ, the better your judgement will be, the opposite appears to be true *because* intelligence can give you a false sense of security about your ability to make great decisions in all domains (unrelated to the domains in which you are legitimately an expert), help you build substantive wealth - which may increase your risk taking and sense of invulnerability, and increase your success and status (due to successful career) that can lead to hubris in unrelated (to one’s career) areas of life. Jonathan Haidt writes about this in The Righteous mind - in that your intellect functions more like a lawyer defending whatever you want to believe than a scientist searching for the truth. So the smarter you are, the better you are at defending what you want to believe, not necessarily finding the truth/reality. Recent examples in the world of AI include: * Some Chat GPT staff (presumably some of the brightest people on earth), ignore the knowledge built up over time - and preserved over centuries in our cultures and civil and religious institutions - about behaviors and lifestyles that lead to lives full of meaning and purpose. If consensual non-consent was a path towards building a life full of meaning and purpose, we would presumably have large-scale institutions established and thriving devoted to (or at least including) this lifestyle. https://x.com/aaronsibarium/status/2122361245357478085 * Jensen Huang affairs: https://x.com/washingtonpost/status/2120879597931651429 One if the brightest and capable leaders of our age, Jensen apparently failed to recognize - or just ignored - how having affairs could/would likely be devastating to his marriage *and* how having an affair with a Chinese woman with whom one is doing business could be portrayed as a conflict of interest and risking US security and impacting the sale of chips to China, etc, etc, etc. So where does this leave intelligent people? I don’t know the best answers, but recognizing your intelligence can just make you better at defending what you want (and fooling yourself) is probably a good place to start when emotions, wants, needs, and desires are involved. Also, a little humility probably goes along way in recognizing the behaviors, traditions, and practices preserved in our culture and institutions - they are there and survived for a reason - because they improve people’s lives at scale. In other words, proceed at your own risk when placing your IQ above the preserved knowledge in our cultures and institutions when decisions involving wants, needs, and desires are involved. Also see this Engagement Hierarchy as some of this may be relevant : [private link removed] to this topic…specifically that there are different kind of knowledge and being great at level 2 may not help you at level 3. Not really sure where to land this, but maybe something along the lines of the highest level of intelligence may be learning to recognize when to subjugate your own intellect to the accumulated wisdom embodied in our cultures, traditions, and institutions …. At least if your priority is living a high-quality life.
